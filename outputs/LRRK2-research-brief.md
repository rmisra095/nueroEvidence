# LRRK2 and Parkinson’s — reference brief

Five selected studies; abstract-level evidence; not a complete or current treatment review. Reviewed October 6, 2026.

## What supports LRRK2?

Certain LRRK2 mutations segregated with inherited parkinsonism in families. [Zimprich et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15541309/) — C01

G2019S was present in 8 of 482 patients in this study of idiopathic Parkinson’s disease. [Gilks et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15680457/) — C02

Pathogenic LRRK2 variants increased Rab protein phosphorylation in laboratory experiments. [Steger et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26824392/) — C04

## What are the limits?

This study estimated Parkinson’s risk in G2019S carriers at 28% by age 59, 51% by 69, and 74% by 79. [Healy et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18539534/) — C03

The reported G2019S risk estimates remain below 100% through the ages studied; carrying the mutation does not guarantee disease by those ages. [Healy et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18539534/) — C06 (Interpretation)

DNL201 inhibited LRRK2 and was well tolerated at doses demonstrating pathway engagement in phase 1 and 1b trials. [Jennings et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35675433/) — C05

The DNL201 target-inhibition and tolerability finding does not establish that the drug slows Parkinson’s. [Jennings et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35675433/) — C07 (Interpretation)

## What remains unanswered?

Research questions prompted by this collection; not established findings.

- Does inhibiting LRRK2 slow Parkinson’s progression?
- Which patients might benefit, with or without particular LRRK2 mutations?
- Does changing Rab phosphorylation improve disease outcomes?
